## Sheet 1 — retained v1

Reference order: helicopter, SAM, relay, hen.

```text
Use case: illustration-story. Create a professional cinematic storyboard sheet for the opening of the painterly anthropomorphic tactics game Animal Factory Tactics. This is a new storyboard, not an edit of the references.

REFERENCE ROLES: Image 1 is the existing olive utility helicopter design; use ONLY its intact upper-row aircraft, ignore the wrecks and truck. Image 2 is the existing intact SAM site on the LEFT: two ivory missiles with red noses on two olive launch rails, round pedestal, square concrete pad and control box. Ignore its destroyed version. Image 3 is the radio/relay station design: red-and-white lattice mast with dishes and a little olive equipment hut, use its INTACT left side only. Image 4 is the exact hen character design: round russet hen, red comb and small wattles, gold beak, olive waistcoat and cream apron, feathered wings. Do not turn her into a rooster or human.

STYLE: readable production storyboard drawings, graphite and confident ink linework with selective gouache colour, earthy olive/ochre terrain, warm grey sky, rusty reds; the same painterly game aesthetic as references. Cinematic compositions with clean silhouettes, restrained detail. NO browser UI, no isometric diagram, no photorealism. Landscape canvas, six large panels in an even THREE COLUMNS by TWO ROWS grid, slim cream gutters, wide cinematic panel interiors. Uncluttered black panel numbers and short captions. Keep all captions fully within the canvas.

TITLE exactly: "INTRO — APPROACH & AMBUSH"
Subtitle exactly: "STORYBOARD · SHOTS 01–06"
Reading order left to right, top row then bottom row. Every exterior view keeps helicopter travel screen LEFT to RIGHT, nose pointing RIGHT. A broad grass plateau with low exposed rock faces lies ahead to the RIGHT; this is a landscape terrace, not a towering mountain. The relay mast is on top of the plateau. Consistent daytime lighting and same helicopter in all panels.

Panel 01, caption "01  APPROACH": wide rear-side tracking shot of intact olive helicopter airborne at left, nose RIGHT, approaching the broad grassy plateau at right. Small red-and-white relay mast visible ahead on the plateau. Rotor blurred in flight, intact skids and tail.
Panel 02, caption "02  THE MISSION": cut INSIDE the helicopter cabin, medium close-up of the reference hen in her olive waistcoat and cream apron. She is speaking calmly into a small handheld wired microphone held naturally by the tips of one feathered wing. She has a simple shoulder restraint. Keep microphone close to her beak and an aircraft window behind her. One legible speech balloon containing EXACTLY: "Our mission is to take out the central relay station."
Panel 03, caption "03  CLOSING IN": cut back OUTSIDE to a closer side tracking shot of the same intact helicopter moving RIGHT toward the plateau. Relay mast, dish and equipment hut are clearer ahead. A smooth stable flight pose before the attack.
Panel 04, caption "04  SAM WAKES": hard cut to the intact reference SAM site on the plateau. Twin loaded rails pivot upward toward upper LEFT; small mechanical motion strokes and a control-box lamp coming on show it whirring to life. No launch yet. Keep both missiles visible on their two rails.
Panel 05, caption "05  FIRST LAUNCH": the first of the TWO missiles launches toward upper LEFT, with a sharp orange-white exhaust and pale smoke. Show one missile already leaving its rail and ONE remaining loaded missile on the other rail. The pedestal stays grounded.
Panel 06, caption "06  SECOND LAUNCH": the second missile launches immediately after the first, leaving BOTH launch rails empty. Wide enough to see two distinct staggered missile trails headed up and left toward the approaching distant helicopter, which is still traveling screen RIGHT. Two launches in sequence, never duplicate or replenish the launcher. No hit until the next sheet.

Do not depict casualties, gore, destroyed relay, cabin explosion or damaged helicopter yet. Prioritize camera cuts, clear sequence and consistency over ornamental detail.
```

## Sheet 2 — initial draft, superseded

Reference order: retained sheet 1, actual survivable crash capture, helicopter design sheet. The initial draft remains in local task artifacts. It was corrected for incoming missile direction, flare attitude and debris at the cabin exit.

```text
Use case: illustration-story. Create the SECOND matching sheet of the cinematic intro storyboard for Animal Factory Tactics. This is a NEW six-panel storyboard, not an edit to sheet 1.

REFERENCE ROLES: Image 1 is the APPROVED FIRST storyboard sheet. Match its painterly ink/gouache style, cream gutters, lettering style, olive UH-1-like helicopter, broad grassy plateau, daylight and relay mast. Continue its screen direction: helicopter nose always points SCREEN RIGHT, tail SCREEN LEFT. Image 2 is the ACTUAL SURVIVABLE CRASH ASSET: ignore browser UI and grid but preserve the final mostly upright cabin, ATTACHED tail, crushed skids, one snapped main blade and one bent main blade, open side door and small engine smoke plume. This is the essential ending reference. Image 3 is the helicopter design sheet; use its UPPER intact aircraft as the starting silhouette, DO NOT use its detached-tail or rolled-over wrecks.

Same landscape format as image 1. Exactly SIX panels, THREE COLUMNS by TWO ROWS, wide cinematic framing, cream caption strips, clean black panel numbers. Fine graphite/ink construction strokes, selective gouache earth colours and readable action silhouettes. Restrained orange only in the missile impact. No gore, no casualties, no total aircraft fireball. Leave the central cabin recognizably intact.

TITLE exactly: "INTRO — HIT & FORCED LANDING"
Subtitle exactly: "STORYBOARD · SHOTS 07–12"

Panel 07, caption "07  TAIL STRIKE": medium-wide side shot of helicopter airborne and pointing RIGHT. A localized orange flash strikes the TAIL BOOM on the LEFT, just aft of the cabin and WELL AWAY from the cockpit. A few small metal fragments, damaged tail-control surfaces and clipped tail rotor. The tail remains ATTACHED. No explosion around passengers. The main rotor remains in one piece and spinning.
Panel 08, caption "08  LOSING CONTROL": wider tracking shot of the same aircraft yawing and descending toward an open grassy clearing BELOW the plateau. Tail and fin attached but damaged, a narrow smoke ribbon from the tail hit, slight bank and nose just above level. Forward movement remains LEFT TO RIGHT; no full spin, no inversion, no giant plunge. Keep the plateau and relay in the distance behind it.
Panel 09, caption "09  FLARE": low side view, the pilot raises the nose slightly as both skids approach the ground. The intact cabin is still entirely airborne, the skids have NOT broken yet. Strong ground shadow immediately below establishes the small remaining height. Main rotor is spinning. Show the clearing ahead and the plateau beyond.
Panel 10, caption "10  SKIDS GIVE WAY": low close side view of the FIRST ground contact. The front/underside skids compress and buckle visibly against soil under the helicopter's weight while the airframe continues sliding RIGHT. Dust, two shallow furrows and a few small soil clods. The cabin remains upright, the tail attached. Main rotor still above the aircraft. Clearly show ground contact and weight, not a floating wreck.
Panel 11, caption "11  ROTOR STRIKE": wider three-quarter view of the short violent rock onto one collapsed skid during the skid-out. The rotor disk tilts and flexes enough that its LOW TIP visibly catches the ground off to the side; the tip breaks away in a small spray of blade fragments and soil. The mast bends; the opposing blade bends. The cabin DOES NOT roll over or explode. Emphasize the actual tip-to-ground contact with a small white impact stroke, not a fiery explosion. Nose still points RIGHT. This is an intermediate rock before settling upright.
Panel 12, caption "12  THE CRASH SITE": quiet wide three-quarter shot matching image 2's survivable asset. Aircraft has settled mostly upright on crushed skids, ATTACHED punctured tail, one short snapped blade and one long bent blade, rotor STOPPED. Grounded shed door/blade fragments are on the FAR side; the near side cabin doorway and clear exit lane are open and unobstructed. Thin dark smoke from the damaged engine, fading dust and skid furrows. The distant relay station remains upright on the plateau, still the objective. No new characters needed. Leave visual space beside the door for the squad's eventual exit.

Read these as a consecutive physically motivated sequence: tail damage -> controlled desperate descent -> flare -> skid collapse -> rotor ground strike -> stable survivable wreck. Never detach the tail, burn the cabin, add wheels, change the aircraft type or reverse its travel direction. Keep damage accumulation consistent. Captions must be exactly as given and fully legible.
```
