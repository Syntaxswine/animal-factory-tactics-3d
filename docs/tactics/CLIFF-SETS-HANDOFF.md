# Wall-height cliff sets

## Water-bottom variant and upper-ground clarification

The user clarified that **an impassable cliff face does not prevent units from standing or moving on usable upper ground behind it**. Treat upper-ground support and climbing an edge as separate decisions. This supersedes the historical blanket wording about crags having no upper floor. The rough sloping face itself is not a landing pad, but adjoining suitable upper terrain remains usable. The art study does not implement those gameplay decisions.

Open `cliff-study.html?base=water` for the water-bottom variant, or switch **At the foot** between Grass and Water. It covers both cliff families, joined patches, every river contour variant, the atlas and the original pieces. The land and water variants share exactly the same cliff positions and upper-ground paint. Water stays at Y=0; wall-height tops stay at Y=2. Ground-level reference figures and the sand demonstration patch are hidden in water view. Water motion can be paused and defaults off for reduced-motion preferences.

`cliff-water.js` reuses the existing `foliage/river-water.png` paint and `makePeriodic`, with a cool green tint for separation from the earth. A six-second loop gently distorts the surface. A subtle broken contact ripple follows the actual rock foot: bottom-triangle boundary extraction cancels internal edges, supports transformed roots, and feeds a localized distance raster. It follows both banks of the winding cut without drawing foam on internal tile joins. `{water:true}` opts `createCliff` and `createCliffTiles` into a narrow wet-rock band; it does not alter their geometry or traversal descriptors.

For rendering integration, load the shared water texture once with `loadCliffWaterTexture()`, create a water surface, and call `setCliffs(meshes)` after assembling and positioning the cliff meshes. `update(seconds)` advances the loop; `dispose()` releases surface resources while the caller owns the shared source texture. The preview surface and contact raster cover 40×40 units; production should use a bounded water region appropriate to the map. No saved-map prop, swim rule, climb-from-water route or collision change is included. In particular, a climbable face alone does not authorize a route whose approach is submerged.

Independent hostile review: **9/10**. Fifteen focused tests pass, including unchanged wet/dry geometry, exact wall height, transformed and original-piece water boundaries, exclusion of internal joins, distance-raster accuracy, loop phase and resource ownership. Independent browser review includes native/reverse gorge views, twelve rebuild cycles without errors or resource growth, and rendered frames immediately either side of the loop boundary with a maximum RGB change of 1. The 38-case browser matrix passes with zero errors, along with dry-view restoration, mobile layout, reduced-motion defaults, and six additional water-toggle cycles at a stable 14 geometries / 8 textures. Rendered motion changes 167,607 pixels while the loop boundary differs by at most one RGB level. The 3D distribution build includes the new material/module and existing water source. `tools/check-cliff-water.mjs` provides the repeatable browser checks; use `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` and optional `CLIFF_STUDY_URL` as with the other cliff tools.

## Latest: joined families and independent sand paint

The continuous tile kit now assembles **ledge and crag in one welded landform**. Pass `mixed` to `createCliffTiles` / `cliffTileGeometry` and give each tile `set: 'ledge'` or `set: 'crag'`. All existing river corner masks and contour variants remain valid. Ledge support remains exactly Y=2.00. Adjacent crag vertices meet that datum at the shared edge, then ease down into the broken crown; away from ledges the approved original crag heights remain unchanged. Even an isolated crag surrounded by ledges retains interior relief. This supersedes the earlier assumption that every exposed crag lip must be Y=0.60: a lip beside a ledge must rise to meet it.

Build connected landforms as **one tile collection**. Both neighboring family and paint values are needed before heights and blends are generated. Independently generated single-family chunks do not automatically gain these transitions. The original standalone block previews remain historical pieces; mixed-family assembly uses the continuous tile kit.

Sand is independent of shape. Optional tile `sand` values range from 0 (grass) to 1 (sand); shared corner weights blend the paint smoothly through neighboring tiles. Omitting it gives the preview convention of grassy ledges and sandy crags. Changing paint never changes positions, normals, rim geometry, rubble classification or traversal descriptors. Occupied tiles alone appear in `userData.traversal`, including their masks and family. These are family eligibility descriptors, **not generated body-sized landing pads or active climb routes**. Crags retain no climbing route even where their boundary meets a ledge.

`painted-sand.js` owns the warm, chipped sand paint. New assets under `dist/assets/environment/sand-cliff/`:

- `sand.png`: seamless opaque 512px ground tile covering 2×2 world units.
- `sand-feather.png`: 512px RGBA patch fading to fully transparent edges, with no baked grass colour.
- `tiles.json`: dimensions, colour space, usage and bake validation.

`sand-ground.html` provides both downloads and the same transparent patch over three grass palettes. The solid tile is for broad areas; the feathered sprite is an isolated patch rather than a repeating boundary atlas. `tools/bake-cliff-sand.mjs` regenerates both from the material, checking borders, alpha and identical ground/raised output. Use `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` when Playwright is not installed in the repository and `CLIFF_STUDY_URL` to override the default local server on port 4435.

The live grass material exposes a shared grass tint on ground and caps. Sand blends against that actual tinted grass, so the transition follows local meadow, cool-green or dry colours instead of introducing a fixed green fringe. Sand on steep surfaces uses normal-weighted projections to prevent vertical paint streaks. The approved exposed-soil line remains on grassy rims; its width accounts for slope, and it fades out with sand ownership to avoid a dark cord across bare rock.

The study defaults to **Joined ledge & crag**. Top paint controls demonstrate grass, sand and a blend independently of shape; grass-colour controls affect both elevations. A separate sand patch on the lower ground demonstrates its reuse. Wall height, horse size, river contours and gameplay/editor scope remain unchanged.

## Art and geometry contract

`cliff-models.js` provides `createCliff(set, shape, seed)`, its matching `cliffLayout`, and independently inspectable geometry. All dimensions use the approved prototype units. Ground is Y=0; the flat cap and the highest broken crest are exactly `DIMENSIONS.wall`, currently **2.00**. The animal is not resized.

Each family has straight (4×2 tiles), corner (3×3 L, five occupied tiles), and end (2×2 tiles) pieces. Straight sections share matching end profiles across seed variations. Pieces are closed solids with chipped outlines, recessed strata and painted-style mineral colour. The flat set has a contrasting soil cap; the crag has exposed, sloping stone all the way across the crown. Both support ordinary root rotation. The entire rectangular corner bounding box is not occupied: use the supplied five-cell footprint.

The ledge alone exposes `walkable` cell-centre positions and a local +Z climbing socket containing approach, lip and landing positions. The lip sits inward from the chipped edge. The original standalone crag exposes no authored standing positions on its rough crest and no climbing socket; that is not a blanket prohibition on usable upper ground behind an impassable face. These are authored integration descriptors, not an engine rule inferred from a colour or mesh normal.

`cliff-study.html` compares both sets with the approved horse and a real 2-unit wall. Controls include three shapes, separate or paired sets, seed variations, grey forms, wireframe, guides, orbit and native 58px/unit scale.

## Integration boundary

This drop is an environment-art study. It does not register new saved-map props or change existing maps, movement, AP, editor catalogs, climb animation or ray collision. The viewer explicitly marks that boundary. No ladder or tower code is changed.

The engine's ordinary level spacing is **2.12**, while the user specified wall height **2.00**. Do not raise these cliffs to a floor level or float characters 0.12 above their caps. Integration needs an explicit physical support-height adapter and traversal endpoints at Y=2.00. Preserve the existing logical level rules and resolve that offset deliberately before enabling placement/playtest. Flat pieces need grounded support, blocked rock volume, upper standing cells and a validated climb edge. Crags need blocked rock volume and no climb link across the impassable face. This does not forbid standing or moving on usable upper ground behind that face; support and face traversal must be evaluated separately. Use actual mesh surfaces or an agreed collision approximation rather than silently filling the empty corner notch.

## Continuous landform revision

The original crag now falls farther toward its edges. The expanded kit in `cliff-tiles.js` uses the **same `shoreField` and `shoreMask` functions as the river tiles**, including NW=1, NE=2, SE=4, SW=8 and all three contour variants. There are **16 masks × 3 variants per family** (96 combinations total). Mask 0 is empty and mask 15 fills the tile; inner/outer curves, straight shorelines, saddles, inlets and connected interiors follow the river field rather than an independently approximated outline.

`cliffTileGeometry(set, tiles)` takes integer world-grid `x,z`, `mask`, and `variant`. It rejects duplicate tiles, invalid inputs and inconsistent adjacent corner bits. Each world tile remains one unit. Fine clipped triangles follow the contour; vertices are welded across the whole patch before outer skirts are built, removing internal walls. The original standalone blocks remain in the viewer under Original pieces.

The flat cap stays at exactly Y=2. Crags fall to Y=0.60 at the exposed contour, with irregular sloping crown planes and a Y=2 maximum envelope. Heights are computed in shared world coordinates, so variants join on both axes without independent per-tile peak scaling. A small boundary fragment need not itself contain a highest crest. No horizontal standing surface is assigned to a crag.

The viewer now defaults to a broad plateau and also includes a winding cut, long escarpment, and 16-pattern atlas with A/B/C switching. Swath previews mix variants and show large filled interiors. Atlas order is 0–15 left to right, top to bottom. When building additional chunks, retain world-coordinate tile positions; choose a shared connected tile collection for each mesh to avoid internal crop walls. New terrain metadata labels the family but does not invent automatic climb routes across narrow contour slivers. Integration must derive safe body-sized support and validated edges from these actual surfaces.

## Meadow grass and edge-rock revision

Saved the approved cliff-cap colour recipe as **Cliff meadow grass** (`grass-cliff-meadow`): four 512px PNG tiles, a 1024px 2×2 atlas and `tiles.json` under `dist/assets/environment/grass-cliff-meadow/`. Each tile covers 2×2 world units. All four variants can neighbor one another **without rotation**. These are unlit sRGB base colours; the renderer supplies lighting and shadows. `grass-meadow.html` provides individual downloads, the atlas and a mixed-tile preview.

`painted-grass.js` owns the shared material. It samples the approved grass recipe at its original height internally but places the pattern using horizontal world coordinates, so raising the surface does not change its colour or pattern. Cliff caps and the lower ground now use this same material. Interior colour variations blend without distorting the grass marks; all variant boundaries are common. `tools/bake-meadow-grass.mjs` regenerates the assets from the live shader, verifies border pixels, and verifies identical 512px output at Y=0 and Y=2. Run against the local study server with an installed Playwright package, or provide its module location through `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE`.

`cliff-edge-rocks.js` adds optional decorative stones to the assembled terrain rim metadata. Ledges have sparse low pebbles and gaps; impassable crags have denser, sharper rubble. The cliff mesh and 2-unit cap datum are unchanged. Small stones can project above a ledge as decoration. Rocks align with the actual support normal, embed 2.5cm and are rejected if any actual bottom vertex floats more than 1.5cm or embeds more than 6cm. Ground stones must remain outside the cliff at every bottom vertex. Original standalone pieces are not decorated, preserving their authored climb sockets.
